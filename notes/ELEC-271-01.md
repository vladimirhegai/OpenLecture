## 1. What binary is

*Sources: Lecture 1 Introduction, positional number representation and binary numbers.pdf, p. 30; Lecture 1 Introduction, positional number representation and binary numbers.pdf, p. 31*

Normal (decimal) numbers use ten digits, 0 to 9. Each place is worth **10 times** the place to its right.

$$352 = 3(100) + 5(10) + 2(1)$$

Binary is the same idea with only two digits, 0 and 1. Each place is worth **2 times** the place to its right.

So the places are worth 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, … going left. After the point they keep halving: ½, ¼, ⅛, …

| Place | $2^4$ | $2^3$ | $2^2$ | $2^1$ | $2^0$ | $2^{-1}$ | $2^{-2}$ | $2^{-3}$ | $2^{-4}$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Worth | 16 | 8 | 4 | 2 | 1 | 0.5 | 0.25 | 0.125 | 0.0625 |

*Sources: Lecture 1 Introduction, positional number representation and binary numbers.pdf, p. 31*

The point sits between the $2^0$ place and the $2^{-1}$ place. Learn this row; you use it in every question.

- **Bit:** one binary digit, 0 or 1.
- **MSB:** the leftmost bit (worth the most). **LSB:** the rightmost bit (worth the least).
- $(1011)_2$ means 1011 in base 2. $(1011)_{10}$ would be one thousand and eleven.

*Sources: Lecture 1 Introduction, positional number representation and binary numbers.pdf, p. 33; Lecture 1 Introduction, positional number representation and binary numbers.pdf, p. 36*

## 2. Binary → decimal

*Sources: Lecture 1 Introduction, positional number representation and binary numbers.pdf, p. 31*

**Method:** write each place's worth above its bit. Add up the worths that have a 1 under them.

| Worth | 8 | 4 | 2 | 1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bit | 1 | 0 | 1 | 1 |

$$(1011)_2 = 8 + 2 + 1 = 11$$

Fractions work the same way. The bits after the point are worth 0.5, 0.25, 0.125, …

| Worth | 8 | 4 | 2 | 1 | 0.5 | 0.25 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bit | 1 | 0 | 1 | 0 | 0 | 1 |

*Sources: Lecture 1 Introduction, positional number representation and binary numbers.pdf, p. 31*

$$(1010.01)_2 = 8 + 2 + 0.25 = 10.25$$

The formula sheet writes the same idea as a sum. You don't need it if you use the table above.

$$V=\sum_{i=-m}^{n-1} b_i\,k^i$$

*Sources: ELEC 271 Midterm Formula Sheet.pdf, p. 1*

- $k$ = the base (2 for binary, 16 for hex).
- $b_i$ = the digit in position $i$. Position 0 is just left of the point.
- Positions after the point are negative: $-1, -2, \dots$

**Try it: Tutorial 2 Q1.6 and a fraction**

Convert to decimal: (a) $(11100)_2$ (b) $(111111)_2$ (c) $(101010101010)_2$ (d) $(110.101)_2$

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

1. (a) The 1s sit in the 16, 8 and 4 places.

   $$16+8+4=28$$

2. (b) Six 1s in a row is one less than the next place (64). Adding every place also works.

   $$64-1=63$$

3. (c) The 1s sit in places 11, 9, 7, 5, 3, 1.

   $$2048+512+128+32+8+2=2730$$

4. (d) Use 0.5, 0.25, 0.125 after the point.

   $$4+2+0.5+0.125=6.625$$

**Answer:** 28, 63, 2730, 6.625.

</details>

## 3. Decimal → binary

*Sources: Lecture 1 Introduction, positional number representation and binary numbers.pdf, p. 37; Lecture 1 Introduction, positional number representation and binary numbers.pdf, p. 38; Lecture 1 Introduction, positional number representation and binary numbers.pdf, p. 39*

Split the number at the point. The whole part and the fraction part use **different** methods. We'll do last year's midterm question as the example.

**Past midterm Q2a:** convert $14.3125$ to binary.

*Sources: ELEC_271_Fall2025_Midterm.pdf, p. 3*

### Whole part: keep dividing by 2

1. **Divide** the number by 2. Write down the remainder (always 0 or 1).
2. **Repeat** with the quotient until the quotient is 0.
3. **Read** the remainders from the **bottom up**.

| Operation | Quotient | Remainder |
|---|---|---|
| 14 ÷ 2 | 7 | 0 |
| 7 ÷ 2 | 3 | 1 |
| 3 ÷ 2 | 1 | 1 |
| 1 ÷ 2 | 0 | 1 |

*Sources: ELEC_271_Fall2025_Midterm.pdf, p. 3*

Bottom up: $14 = (1110)_2$. Check: $8+4+2=14$.

Why bottom up? The first remainder tells you if the number is odd, which is the 1s place (the rightmost bit). Each division moves you one place further left.

### Fraction part: keep multiplying by 2

1. **Multiply** the fraction by 2.
2. The digit in front of the point (0 or 1) is your **next bit**.
3. **Drop** that digit, keep only the part after the point, and repeat.
4. **Stop** when you reach exactly 0 (or have as many bits as the question asks). Read the bits **top down**.

| Operation | Result | Bit |
|---|---|---|
| 0.3125 × 2 | 0.625 | 0 |
| 0.625 × 2 | 1.25 | 1 |
| 0.25 × 2 | 0.5 | 0 |
| 0.5 × 2 | 1.0 | 1 |

*Sources: ELEC_271_Fall2025_Midterm.pdf, p. 3*

Top down: $0.3125 = (.0101)_2$.

Why it works: multiplying by 2 slides the binary point one place right, so the first bit after the point pops out in front.

$$14.3125 = (1110.0101)_2$$

**Always check** by going back: $8+4+2+0.25+0.0625 = 14.3125$ ✓

**Try it: Tutorial 2 Q1.1 and a lecture fraction**

Convert to binary: (a) $20$ (b) $100$ (c) $129$ (d) $23.375$

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

1. (a) Remainders 0,0,1,0,1, read bottom up.

   $$20=(10100)_2$$

2. (b) Remainders 0,0,1,0,0,1,1, read bottom up.

   $$100=(1100100)_2$$

3. (c) $129 = 128 + 1$, so only the 128 and 1 places get a 1.

   $$129=(10000001)_2$$

4. (d) Whole part: $23 = 16+4+2+1$. Fraction: $0.375 \times 2 = 0.75$ → 0, $0.75 \times 2 = 1.5$ → 1, $0.5\times 2=1.0$ → 1.

   $$23.375=(10111.011)_2$$

**Answer:** 10100, 1100100, 10000001, 10111.011.

</details>

*Sources: Tutorial Week 2.pdf, p. 1*

> **Quick check trick:** for a small number, find the biggest place worth that fits, subtract it, and repeat. $23$: take 16 (7 left), take 4 (3 left), take 2 (1 left), take 1. So the 16, 4, 2, 1 places are 1: $10111$.

## 4. Extras: fractions that never end, and how many bits

*Sources: Lecture 1 Introduction, positional number representation and binary numbers.pdf, p. 40*

What if the fraction never reaches 0? Try $0.2$:

| Operation | Result | Bit |
|---|---|---|
| 0.2 × 2 | 0.4 | 0 |
| 0.4 × 2 | 0.8 | 0 |
| 0.8 × 2 | 1.6 | 1 |
| 0.6 × 2 | 1.2 | 1 |
| 0.2 × 2 | … | repeats |

*Sources: Lecture 1 Introduction, positional number representation and binary numbers.pdf, p. 40*

We're back at $0.2$, so the bits repeat forever: $0.2 = (.001100110011\ldots)_2$.

**Rule:** a fraction ends in binary only if its bottom number is a power of 2. $\tfrac38$ ends ($8=2^3$). $\tfrac15$ and $\tfrac23$ never end.

**Past midterm Q2b: how close do four bits get?**

Write $2/3$ in binary to four places. Convert that back to decimal. How close is it to $2/3$?

1. Multiply by 2 four times. The fraction bounces between 0.666… and 0.333…

   $$1.333\to1,\quad 0.666\to0,\quad 1.333\to1,\quad 0.666\to0$$

2. Keep the four bits.

   $$\tfrac23 \approx (.1010)_2$$

3. Convert back: the 1s are in the 0.5 and 0.125 places.

   $$0.5+0.125=0.625$$

4. Subtract to get the error.

   $$0.6667-0.625\approx0.0417$$

**Answer:** $(.1010)_2 = 0.625$, which is about $0.0417$ below $2/3$.

*Sources: ELEC_271_Fall2025_Midterm.pdf, p. 3*

### How many bits do I need?

With $n$ bits you can count from $0$ to $2^n-1$. So find the **first power of 2 that is bigger** than your number. That power is your bit count.

$$1029:\quad 2^{10}=1024 \le 1029 < 2048=2^{11}\quad\Rightarrow\quad 11\text{ bits}$$

*Sources: Tutorial Week 2.pdf, p. 1*

Tutorial 2 Q1.8 for practice: $270$ → 9 bits (512), $520$ → 10 bits (1024), $780$ → 10 bits (1024).

## 5. Hexadecimal

*Sources: Lecture 1 Introduction, positional number representation and binary numbers.pdf, p. 41; Lecture 1 Introduction, positional number representation and binary numbers.pdf, p. 42; Lecture 1 Introduction, positional number representation and binary numbers.pdf, p. 43*

Binary numbers get long fast, so we use hex as shorthand. Hex is base 16, and $16 = 2^4$, so **one hex digit is exactly 4 bits**.

Hex needs 16 digits. After 9 it uses letters: A = 10, B = 11, C = 12, D = 13, E = 14, F = 15.

| Hex | Binary | Hex | Binary |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0000 | 8 | 1000 |
| 1 | 0001 | 9 | 1001 |
| 2 | 0010 | A (10) | 1010 |
| 3 | 0011 | B (11) | 1011 |
| 4 | 0100 | C (12) | 1100 |
| 5 | 0101 | D (13) | 1101 |
| 6 | 0110 | E (14) | 1110 |
| 7 | 0111 | F (15) | 1111 |

*Sources: Lecture 1 Introduction, positional number representation and binary numbers.pdf, p. 41*

This table is on the formula sheet, so you don't need to memorize it.

*Sources: ELEC 271 Midterm Formula Sheet.pdf, p. 1*

### Binary → hex: group by 4 from the point

1. **Start at the point.** Group bits in fours going **left** for the whole part and **right** for the fraction.
2. **Pad with zeros on the outside ends** if a group is short (far left, or far right).
3. **Swap** each group of 4 for its hex digit.

$$(10110110.101)_2 \;\to\; 1011\;0110\,.\,1010 \;\to\; (\mathrm{B6.A})_{16}$$

*Sources: Lecture 1 Introduction, positional number representation and binary numbers.pdf, p. 42; Lecture 1 Introduction, positional number representation and binary numbers.pdf, p. 43*

Why pad on the outside? $.101$ and $.1010$ are the same number, just like $0.5 = 0.50$. Padding the inside, $.0101$, would change the value.

### Hex → binary: swap each digit for 4 bits

$$(2\mathrm{F}.4)_{16} \;\to\; 0010\;1111\,.\,0100 \;\to\; (101111.01)_2$$

Drop the leading zeros on the left and the trailing zeros on the right at the end.

### Hex → decimal: places are worth 16, 1, 1/16

Same idea as binary, but each place is worth 16 times the one to its right.

$$(2\mathrm{F}.4)_{16} = 2(16) + 15(1) + \tfrac{4}{16} = 32+15+0.25 = 47.25$$

### Decimal → hex: go through binary

**Decimal to hex with a fraction**

Convert $200.75$ to hexadecimal.

1. Whole part to binary. $200 = 128+64+8$.

   $$200=(11001000)_2$$

2. Fraction to binary: $0.75\times2=1.5$ → 1, $0.5\times2=1.0$ → 1.

   $$0.75=(.11)_2$$

3. Group by 4 from the point, padding the fraction on the right.

   $$1100\;1000\,.\,1100$$

4. Swap each group for a hex digit.

   $$(\mathrm{C8.C})_{16}$$

5. Check in decimal.

   $$12(16)+8+\tfrac{12}{16}=200.75$$

**Answer:** $(\mathrm{C8.C})_{16}$.

**Try it: both directions**

(a) Convert $(3\mathrm{A}.8)_{16}$ to decimal and to binary. (b) Convert $45.25$ to hex.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

1. (a) Decimal: A = 10 and the 8 sits in the 1/16 place.

   $$3(16)+10+\tfrac8{16}=58.5$$

2. (a) Binary: swap each digit for 4 bits.

   $$0011\;1010\,.\,1000\to(111010.1)_2$$

3. (b) $45 = 32+8+4+1$ and $0.25 = .01$.

   $$45.25=(101101.01)_2$$

4. (b) Group by 4 from the point.

   $$0010\;1101\,.\,0100\to(\mathrm{2D.4})_{16}$$

**Answer:** (a) $58.5$ and $(111010.1)_2$. (b) $(\mathrm{2D.4})_{16}$.

</details>
