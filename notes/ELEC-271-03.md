## 2. The rule

*Sources: Chapter3_Implementation_technology Lecture 1.pdf, p. 17; Chapter3_Implementation_technology Lecture 1.pdf, p. 24*

- **AND** ($\cdot$) = **series** (one under the other)
- **OR** ($+$) = **parallel** (side by side)

Draw one half **normal** (exactly as the expression says) and the other half **swapped** (series ↔ parallel). Which half is normal depends on the shape of $f$:

| $f$ looks like | PMOS (top) | NMOS (bottom) |
|---|---|---|
| **every letter barred:** $\bar x_1+\bar x_2\bar x_3$ | **normal** | swapped |
| **one bar over plain letters:** $\overline{x_1+x_2x_3}$ | swapped | **normal** |
| **no bars at all:** $x_1+x_2x_3$ | swapped | **normal**, then a NOT on the output |

**Q7 (mixed bars).** Draw a CMOS circuit for $f=\bar x_1+x_2$.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

$x_2$ has no bar, so it doesn't fit either shape. **Fix:** make $y=\bar x_2$ with a NOT gate. Then $x_2=\bar y$ and

$$
f=\bar x_1+\bar y
$$

Now every letter is barred → **PMOS normal, NMOS swapped.**

- PMOS: $x_1$ **parallel** $y$
- NMOS: $x_1$ **series** $y$

```digital
{"kind":"cmos","title":"f = NOT x1 + x2, with y = NOT x2","pdn":{"series":[{"gate":"x1"},{"gate":"y"}]}}
```

*4 transistors + 2 for the NOT gate that makes y = 6.*

</details>

**Q15.** What gate is this? When $x_1=1$, $x_2=0$, what is $f$, and does current flow?

*Sources: Chapter3_Implementation_technology Lecture 1.pdf, p. 13*

```digital
{"kind":"cmos","title":"NMOS gate","pdn":{"parallel":[{"gate":"x1"},{"gate":"x2"}]},"pullup":"resistor"}
```

*Resistor on top, two NMOS below.*

*Sources: Chapter3_Implementation_technology Lecture 1.pdf, p. 13*

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

NMOS read normally: $x_1+x_2$. Bar over it: $f=\overline{x_1+x_2}$, a **NOR**.

$x_1=1$ closes its switch, so $f$ is connected to ground: $f=0$. **Yes**, current flows $V_{DD}$ → resistor → ground. That wasted power is why CMOS uses PMOS instead of a resistor.


*Green = the path the current takes.*

</details>
